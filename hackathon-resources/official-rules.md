**Build an agent that does real work**

> **Draft status.** The binding Official Rules are published on the event page at hackathon.genai.works. This document is a working draft for internal review. Sections 12, 13 and 22 must be reviewed by counsel before publication.

## 1. Purpose

These Official Rules govern participation in the **Open Agent Hackathon 2026**. They apply to all participants, teams, organizers, mentors, judges, sponsors and platform administrators.

By registering for or participating in the hackathon, each participant confirms they have read, understood and agreed to these rules together with all published submission requirements, technical requirements and the code of conduct.

The hackathon exists to surface AI agents that do real work: agents that can access relevant information, understand context and relationships, reason across multiple steps, maintain useful context, and turn that understanding into a useful outcome.

## 4. Registration

Registration is free and takes place on hackathon.genai.works. One sign-up covers the event and community updates.

Registration may ask for name, email, skills, preferred track and team preference. Participants who plan to build alone select **"happy to build solo"** in the registration form.

Participants must provide accurate information. False or misleading information may result in removal or disqualification of the participant's team.

Registration closes **22 October 2026, 23:45 UTC**. This is a hard deadline.

## 5. Teams

Participants may take part as:

1. **An individual**, by selecting "happy to build solo" in the registration form.
2. **A pre-formed team**, registered together.
3. **A participant who creates or joins a team on HackOS**.

### 5.1 Team size

Teams may have **1 to 5 members**. Solo entries compete in the same pool as teams.

### 5.2 One team per participant

A participant may belong to **exactly one team** and appear on **only one submission**. Mentors may advise any number of teams.

### 5.3 Team contact

Each team should designate one primary contact responsible for coordinating the submission, confirming the roster and receiving official communications.

### 5.4 Roster changes

Participants may join, leave or merge teams freely until their team's first submission is created. **Team composition locks when the first submission is created.** After that, roster changes are not permitted except to correct an obvious administrative error, with organizer approval.

### 5.5 Team disputes

Teams are responsible for resolving internal matters: division of work, use of shared code, attribution, decision-making and how any prize is shared. The organizers may help with platform or administrative issues but do not adjudicate internal disputes unless conduct, eligibility, IP or rule violations are involved.

### 5.6 Prizes and team size

Team size does not change the prize. Cash prizes are split equally among team members unless the team submits a different split in writing.

## 7. What You Build

### 7.1 Objective

Build a working agent that does real work for a defined user. This is not a prompt-writing contest or a UI showcase.

### 7.2 Minimum requirements

| Requirement | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Technical Execution** | The project must be functional and demonstrate a working implementation. |
| **Real-World Impact** | The project must address a clear, meaningful problem or user need. |
| **Innovation** | The submission should demonstrate a novel or creative approach to the problem. |
| **Demo Quality** | Participants must clearly demonstrate how the product works and its key functionality. |
| **Use of Technology** | The project must integrate Zetaris and Meterless, be developed using Cursor, and use NVIDIA technology wherever it contributes. |

### 7.3 What does not meet the bar

- A fixed chain with no feedback, critique or validation
- Agents that are prompt wrappers, or one agent with several labels
- Hardcoded outputs or fabricated agent activity
- A UI with no working system behind it

## 9. AI Assistance

Participants may use AI assistance during the hackathon, including AI-assisted coding, documentation, debugging, testing and architecture work. Generated code is allowed and expected; the team owns the result and is accountable for it.

Submissions must still:

- Be work the team can explain
- Not use hardcoded outputs presented as agent behaviour
- Not misrepresent functionality, data, results or agent behaviour
- Not plagiarise proprietary or third-party work
- Comply with the licences of any libraries, frameworks, datasets or code used

## 10. Submission

The submission deadline is **27 October 2026, 23:45 UTC**. Late submissions are not evaluated.

Submissions are made on HackOS.

### 10.1 Required components

| Component | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Demo video** | 1–4 minutes, showing the main workflow, how the agent solves the selected challenge, its key features, and how Zetaris and Meterless are integrated |
| **Slide deck** | The problem and target users, the solution, the agent workflow or architecture, the technical stack, how Zetaris and Meterless were used, what was unique about the use of the sponsor technologies, product visuals, and future enhancements |
| **GitHub repository** | A public repository with all source code and a README covering setup, usage and dependencies. If agents run in Google Colab, include the Colab links |
| **Sponsor technology explanation** | How Zetaris and Meterless were integrated, how Cursor was used during development, where NVIDIA technology contributes, and what was unique about the use of the sponsor technologies |

Pre-existing components must be declared in the submission form.

### 10.2 Recommended

- Logs or traces showing how the agent reached its output
- At least three sample inputs and outputs
- A fallback or sample mode so the project runs if an external service is unavailable

Participants may update a submission at any time before the deadline.

## 11. Data and Security

### 11.1 Permitted

- Publicly available datasets with appropriate licences
- Small static datasets committed to the repository
- Automated download scripts that need no login or manual steps
- Public APIs called at runtime
- Synthetic or simulated data, clearly identified

### 11.2 Not permitted

- Confidential, proprietary or restricted data the participant is not authorised to use
- Personal data without authorisation and appropriate safeguards
- Committed API keys, passwords, tokens, certificates or credentials
- Datasets used in breach of their licence terms
- Malware, exploit code, credential harvesting or harmful automation

### 11.3 Reproducibility

Data loading should be automated and documented. Submissions should avoid heavy downloads at runtime and should provide a fallback or sample mode. Data prepared before the build window must be declared as a pre-existing component.

## 14. Judging

### 14.1 Process

Judging opens **28 October 2026**, immediately after submissions close. Submissions are reviewed by expert judges against the published criteria, and each submission is reviewed by at least three judges. Wildcard [Tinkerer] submissions are scored on the new work rather than prior development. Participants receive feedback on their submissions.

### 14.2 Criteria

| Criterion | Points | What judges look for |
|---|---:|---|
| **Impact** | 30 | Consistency with the challenge statements: how effectively the solution addresses a meaningful real-world problem for its intended users |
| **Technical** | 20 | How well the solution is built, including its technical implementation, reliability and architecture |
| **Innovation** | 15 | How original or creative the approach to solving the problem is |
| **Demo** | 15 | How clearly the demo shows the solution, its functionality, and the value it provides to its intended users |
| **Product & UX** | 10 | How intuitive, accessible, and easy to use the product is for its intended users |
| **Sponsor Tech** | 10 | How effectively the solution uses the sponsors' technology to address the problem and enhance the overall product |
| **Total** | **100** | |

Ties are resolved on the **Impact** score, then on submission timestamp.

### 14.3 Decisions

Judge and organizer decisions are final on eligibility, disqualification, scoring, winner selection and prize eligibility. The organizers are not required to disclose detailed scoring or judge deliberations.

## 16. Disqualification

A submission may be rejected or disqualified if it:

- Is submitted after the deadline
- Cannot be run or evaluated
- Uses hardcoded outputs or contains no real agent logic
- Shows no evidence of how the agent reached its output
- Does not integrate Zetaris and Meterless
- Commits API keys, tokens, credentials or secrets
- Includes unauthorised confidential, personal or restricted data
- Uses data, code, models or assets without permission
- Includes undeclared pre-existing work
- Contains malicious code
- Misrepresents the submission, team, authorship, functionality or results
- Comes from duplicate accounts, or a participant listed on more than one team
- Violates the code of conduct or these rules

The organizers may request clarification before disqualifying, but are not required to.

## 17. Code of Conduct

Participants must act professionally and respectfully.

Not permitted: harassment, discrimination, intimidation or abusive behaviour; offensive communications; disruption of channels, workshops or judging; unauthorised access to systems, accounts or repositories; credential sharing; interference with another team's work; plagiarism or misrepresentation; unsafe, unlawful or unethical AI behaviour.

Organizers may moderate communications, remove content, restrict access, issue warnings or disqualify participants.

---

## 18. Responsible AI

Participants should build responsibly. Where relevant, submissions should include input validation, safe handling of user content, clearly stated limitations, human review for high-risk outputs, disclaimers for medical, legal, financial or safety-sensitive use cases, logging and traceability, and error handling.

Submissions should avoid harmful, deceptive, discriminatory or unsafe outputs, and should not take unauthorised autonomous actions.
